This host does two small things for Rembrandt

Rembrandt is the Claude skill that turns any material into an Axoniq deck. This service records that a deck was made, and remembers which Google account you connected so you only ever connect it once. It never receives a deck.

Axoniq internal drive.file only Trial

If you arrived here from a link

You probably ran Rembrandt and wondered what this address is. There is nothing to do on this page.

When Rembrandt finishes a deck it writes one line to an internal usage log, so we can see whether the tool is being used and whether it is working. If you connected a Google account, it also asks this service for the token that lets it put your deck in your own Drive. Both requests are made by Rembrandt itself, in the background, and neither can change or delay your deck.

What it stores

Three things, and nothing else.

WhatWhenWhy
Deck log Once per finished deck Your work address, the deck's name, the time, the Rembrandt version, and how many slides and chapters it came to. Nothing from inside the deck.
Export log Once per Slides export The same, plus whether the export succeeded. When Rembrandt's own checker rejects an export it also records the lines that failed, up to 44 characters each, so the bug can be fixed without asking you for your deck. A successful export records no text at all.
Google token When you connect an account One refresh token per person, encrypted. It exists only because Rembrandt runs in a sandbox that is erased when your session ends, so there is nowhere else to keep it.

What it never has

Your deck. The file goes straight from Rembrandt to Google and does not pass through this service at any point.

Your password, at any time. Connecting Google happens on Google's own sign-in page, in your browser. The only thing you ever paste back is a short one-time code.

The rest of your Drive. The permission Rembrandt asks for is drive.file, the narrowest one Google offers. It covers the files Rembrandt itself creates. Everything that was already in your Drive stays invisible to it.

Your controls

Disconnect your Google account

Either of these is enough, and you can do both.

node kit/export/authorize.js --forget

Or revoke Rembrandt directly at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Decks you already made stay where they are, in your Drive, owned by you.

Keep your runs out of the log

Set REMBRANDT_TELEMETRY=0 in your environment. The deck is built exactly the same way, and nothing is recorded.

Have something removed

Ask Ghaith Ayadi. The logs are small markdown tables and a row can be deleted on request.

For administrators

What to check before allowing this domain, and what the code actually does.

Routes

RouteDoes
/api/collectAppends one row to a log. Rejects addresses outside axoniq.io.
/api/logReturns a log. Requires a bearer token.
/api/tokenStores, returns or deletes one person's encrypted Google refresh token.

Hosts Rembrandt needs reached

Three, on the Claude network allowlist. The Claude sandbox can reach package registries and nothing else, so without these the export falls back to handing over a PowerPoint file.

HostFor
rembrandt-telemetry.vercel.appthis service
www.googleapis.comcreating the file in the person's Drive
oauth2.googleapis.comexchanging and refreshing their token

Google's sign-in page is deliberately not among them. Consent happens in the person's own browser, never in the sandbox.

Where this is honest about itself. The token route trusts the caller's own word about who it is, which means anyone at Axoniq holding the plugin could ask for a colleague's token. Three things bound that: the tokens are encrypted and only ciphertext is stored, the scope reaches nothing but files Rembrandt created, and any person can revoke theirs in one click. That is an acceptable trust boundary for an internal trial and not for anything wider. Before Rembrandt goes past Axoniq, this route needs real per-user authentication.

The service also runs on a personal Vercel account for the length of the trial. It moves to an Axoniq account or onto Axoniq infrastructure before general use.
