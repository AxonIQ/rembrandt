How to use Rembrandt

1

Invoke Rembrandt

Type /rembrandt, attach or paste your content, and press enter.

Start a new chat every time. Rembrandt reads the whole conversation, so leftovers from the last deck bleed into the next one.

2

Answer, then leave it

5 to 10 min

Rembrandt needs to know how much editorial liberty it has, and it will ask for anything your source leaves out: dates, names, roles, links.

Answer properly and then let it work. This is the part that takes the time.

3

Get an HTML file

One file holding your presentation and the controls to present it: arrows to move, G for every slide at once, F for full screen.

Opens in any browser, works offline, nothing to install.

How to edit a deck

1

Ask Claude

In the same thread where you made the deck, describe your edits. All of them in one go, or one at a time, whichever suits you.

Rembrandt checks every change against the spec. If something you asked for would break the deck, it says so and lets you decide.

2

Edit inline

Open the HTML and press E, or click the EDIT button. Now you can retype any text on any slide, to reword a line or fix a typo.

3

Share it

Send the HTML file as it is. It needs nothing installed at the other end.

Or ask Claude to turn the slides into a PDF, for anywhere a web page will not do.

🚨

Inline edits are not saved until you press Save. Saving gives you a new HTML file, and that new file is the one to share or to open in another browser. Until you save, your changes exist only in the browser you made them in.

What it is, and what it isn't

Rembrandt is the designer at the end of the process, not the room you write in. Knowing which half is which saves the most time.

Best practices

How to hand it over
  • Start in a text editor. Markdown is king; Notion works too. Write paragraphs, tables, bullet points and lists, and use dividers for pages. Do not get bogged down in layout.
  • Attach raw Excel data, not screenshots. For tables and for graphs alike.
  • Add raw code, not screenshots of code. Markdown, Notion, or even Docs are the best input if your deck has code in it.
  • Use bold and underline to emphasise. That is the signal Rembrandt reads to decide what carries weight on the slide.

What it isn't

Ruled out on purpose
  • A collaboration tool. Finish collaborating on content in Notion, like the lord intended, then export a PDF and hand it to the designer.
  • A layout editor. You can make any edit you want by asking Claude, but you cannot move things yourself the way you would in Slides or PowerPoint. It feels weird. Trust me, it is for your own good 🙏🏼
  • An illustrator. Rembrandt will not add visuals to your slides. If they are in the original file, it will use them.
  • A reviewer. Rembrandt checks your slides for coherence. It does not check your statements for veracity. That is the next iteration.

Using data from Excel

Send the sheet, not a picture of the sheet. This is the single change that most improves what comes back.

What happens to a table

Rembrandt picks a table variant from the shape of your data: comparison, matrix, dense, or definitions, chosen by row count and cell length.

Two columns is not a table, it becomes a versus. Cells full of prose are not a table either, they become cards.

What happens to your numbers

A handful of key figures becomes a numbers grid sized to the count: one hero figure, or a grid of four, six or nine.

A target against an actual is drawn as a gauge or before and after bars, with the hero numeral and a reading line under it. Never two numerals and a caption.

What happens to a chart

Your Excel chart is not embedded. Rembrandt redraws from the series in the master's own style, so what it needs is the numbers behind the picture.

If a value exists only inside a chart image, it becomes a question for you rather than a slide.

Preparing the sheet

  1. One table per range, with a real header row. Merged header cells and stacked title rows above the data are the main reason columns get lost on the way in.
  2. Units in the header, not in every cell. A column called Revenue (€m) beats €1.2m repeated forty times, and it keeps the cells short enough for a readable variant.
  3. Pre-aggregate. A four hundred row export is a data source, not a slide. Send the summary you would actually present and keep the full extract attached underneath it.
  4. Say which figure is the point. Most tables have one number the slide is really about. Naming it is the difference between a table and a hero number.
  5. Keep the file attached even if you paste the numbers. Anything ambiguous then gets checked against the source instead of guessed at.
